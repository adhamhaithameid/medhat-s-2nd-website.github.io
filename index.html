Introduction

📋

Familial Mediterranean Fever (FMF) is a chronic genetic autoinflammatory disease characterized by recurrent, self-limiting attacks of fever and inflammation.

It predominantly affects individuals from Mediterranean and Middle Eastern populations and usually begins in childhood or adolescence. Although FMF is a lifelong condition, early diagnosis and appropriate treatment allow most patients to live normal, productive lives.

🌍

Prevalence

Mediterranean & Middle Eastern populations

👶

Onset

Normal life with proper treatment

02

Pathophysiology & Genetics

🧬

Genetic Basis

FMF is caused by mutations in the MEFV gene, which encodes the protein pyrin. Pyrin plays a key role in regulating the inflammatory response.

Key Mechanism

Mutations lead to uncontrolled activation of the inflammasome and excessive production of inflammatory cytokines.

The disease is usually inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, though symptoms may appear in heterozygous individuals.

Epidemiology & Risk Factors

FMF is more prevalent among individuals of Mediterranean origin, including:

🇸🇦 Arabs
🇹🇷 Turks
🇦🇲 Armenians

Risk Factors

  • Family history of FMF
  • Consanguinity
  • Known MEFV gene mutations
03

Clinical Features

🩺

FMF is characterized by recurrent inflammatory attacks that begin suddenly and resolve spontaneously within 1–3 days. Patients are usually asymptomatic between attacks.

Common Manifestations

🌡️

High-grade Fever

Sudden onset, typically lasting 1-3 days

🤕

Severe Abdominal Pain

Due to peritoneal inflammation

💨

Chest Pain

Due to pleuritis or pericarditis

🦵

Joint Pain/Swelling

Particularly knees and ankles

Less Common Features

Skin rashes Muscle pain
⚡

Triggers of Attacks

FMF attacks may be precipitated by:

😰 Physical or emotional stress
🦠 Infections
😴 Fatigue
💊 Irregular medication use
Note: Attacks can also occur without identifiable triggers.
04

Diagnosis

🔬

FMF is primarily a clinical diagnosis supported by laboratory and genetic investigations.

Clinical Criteria

  • Recurrent febrile episodes
  • Serositis (peritonitis, pleuritis)
  • Tel Hashomer criteria

Laboratory Findings (During Attacks)

  • Elevated ESR
  • Elevated CRP
  • Elevated WBC count
  • Elevated Serum Amyloid A

Genetic Testing

Testing for MEFV mutations supports but does not exclude the diagnosis. A negative genetic test does not rule out FMF.

Complications

⚠️

AA Amyloidosis - The Most Serious Complication

The most serious complication of FMF is secondary (AA) amyloidosis, resulting from persistent elevation of Serum Amyloid A.

1 Chronic Inflammation
→
2 Elevated SAA
→
3 Amyloid Deposition
→
4 Organ Damage

Renal Involvement

Amyloid deposition commonly affects the kidneys, leading to:

  • Proteinuria
  • Edema
  • Progressive renal failure if untreated

06

Treatment

💊

Colchicine - The Cornerstone of FMF Management

Colchicine should be taken daily and lifelong.

✓
Reduces frequency and severity of attacks
✓
Suppresses inflammation
✓
Prevents amyloidosis

During Acute Attacks

Symptomatic treatment with paracetamol or NSAIDs may be used under medical supervision.

FMF Treatment Protocol
📅

Monitoring and Follow-Up

Regular follow-up is essential to assess:

📊 Treatment response
💊 Medication adherence
⚠️ Potential complications

Recommended Monitoring

  • Periodic blood tests
  • Urine analysis for protein
  • Renal function assessment in high-risk patients
🌟

Living with FMF

With proper treatment and follow-up, individuals with FMF can lead a normal life.

💊 Adhere strictly to medication
🔍 Identify and avoid triggers
🏃 Maintain a healthy lifestyle
👨‍⚕️ Seek medical advice before discontinuing treatment

Family Screening & Genetic Counseling

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Why Family Screening Matters

Because FMF is a genetic disease, family screening may be recommended in some cases.

Genetic Counseling Can Help:

  • Understand inheritance patterns
  • Recognize the importance of early diagnosis
  • Make informed family planning decisions
  • Identify at-risk family members

08

Conclusion

📝

Familial Mediterranean Fever is a lifelong genetic disorder that requires continuous management. Early recognition, strict adherence to colchicine therapy, and regular follow-up can prevent complications and preserve quality of life.

Key Takeaways

1 Early diagnosis is crucial
2 Colchicine is the mainstay of treatment
3 Lifelong adherence prevents complications
4 Regular monitoring is essential
